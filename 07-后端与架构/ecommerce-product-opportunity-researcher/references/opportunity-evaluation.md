# Product Opportunity Evaluation

Use this reference when the user wants ranked opportunities, competitor research, margin screening, catalog expansion, or a validation shortlist.

## Decision Context

Start by recording the decision, market, customer, business model, geography, time horizon, available evidence, and acceptable confidence level.

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Decision | The yes, no, ranking, priority, or test decision. |
| Market | Category, geography, currency, and sales channel. |
| Customer | Buyer, user, job, pain, trigger, and current alternative. |
| Constraints | Price, landed cost, budget, MOQ, fulfillment, values, and excluded categories. |
| Evidence | Customer, marketplace, search, community, supplier, competitor, and internal data. |
| Gaps | Missing facts that could change the recommendation. |

## Evidence Ledger

Record source quality before assigning confidence.

| Candidate | Evidence type | Source and URL | Date checked | Geography | Sample or method | Observed signal | Limitation | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | | | | |

Use direct customer evidence first when available. Then compare marketplace, search, community, supplier, and competitor signals. Do not treat virality, one bestseller page, or one keyword metric as proof of durable demand.

## Weighted Scoring Rubric

Score each factor from one to five. Explain any score based mainly on assumptions.

| Factor | Weight | A strong score means |
|---|---:|---|
| Demand strength | 20 | Several recent signals show purchase intent or a recurring problem. |
| Competition opportunity | 15 | Existing offers are weak, fragmented, poorly positioned, or miss a clear segment. |
| Margin potential | 15 | Likely costs leave room for fees, marketing, returns, and profit. |
| Differentiation | 15 | The offer can be meaningfully better without relying on easy-to-copy styling. |
| Sourcing feasibility | 10 | Samples, quality control, MOQ, lead time, and backup suppliers are manageable. |
| Logistics fit | 10 | The item is stable, durable, compact, and practical to fulfill. |
| Brand and audience fit | 5 | The opportunity strengthens the catalog and matches customer expectations. |
| Repeat or expansion value | 5 | Refills, accessories, bundles, variants, or adjacent products are credible. |
| Risk profile | 5 | Compliance, safety, intellectual property, returns, and platform risks are limited. |

Calculate the weighted total on a 100-point scale. Show the weakest factor and the evidence needed to improve it. Treat the score as a prioritization aid, not a forecast.

## Unit Economics

When inputs exist, estimate contribution economics.

`Contribution per unit = selling price - product cost - inbound freight - duty - packaging - fulfillment - marketplace or payment fees - expected returns - advertising allowance.`

Separate:

- Revenue, the selling price collected.
- Gross margin, revenue minus product and direct landed costs.
- Contribution margin, revenue minus variable operating and acquisition costs.
- Net profit, contribution after fixed costs, taxes, and other business expenses.

Use ranges when costs are uncertain. Record supplier quote date, fee assumptions, shipping assumptions, return assumptions, and the input that needs confirmation.

## Risk Gate

Assign a low, medium, or high risk level and name the verification required for:

- Batteries, electronics, chargers, wireless devices, and connected products.
- Ingestibles, supplements, cosmetics, allergens, health claims, and medical claims.
- Children’s products, toys, safety equipment, and products used around animals.
- Flammable, hazardous, temperature-sensitive, or short-shelf-life goods.
- Sizing, fit, breakage, leakage, hygiene, and high-return categories.
- Trademarks, patents, copyright, counterfeit risk, and marketplace approvals.
- Organic, cruelty-free, sustainable, recycled, fair-trade, or other certification claims.
- Customer data, tracking, reviews, testimonials, and privacy permissions.

Do not give legal, medical, regulatory, certification, or safety approval from incomplete information. Mark missing verification as `NEEDS_CONFIRMATION`.

## Confidence Labels

- High confidence. Several current and independent sources agree, and important cost and risk inputs are known.
- Medium confidence. Evidence is promising, but one or more material assumptions remain.
- Low confidence. The opportunity is mostly a hypothesis, evidence is thin, or costs and restrictions are unclear.

## Minimum Validation Plan

For every serious candidate, define:

- The assumption being tested.
- The cheapest credible test.
- The success threshold.
- The time and budget.
- The evidence to collect.
- The proceed, revise, or reject decision.
- The kill criteria that prevent further investment.

Prefer small, reversible tests before large inventory commitments. Suitable tests include a customer interview, waitlist, landing page, preorder interest check, sample review, permitted marketplace research, supplier quote comparison, controlled ad test, or small initial order.

## Premortem

Before recommending a launch, ask why the opportunity failed six months later. Check for:

1. Demand was mistaken for attention.
2. Margin disappeared after fees, returns, fulfillment, and acquisition.
3. A supplier could not meet quality, lead time, or compliance requirements.
4. The offer had no defensible difference from existing alternatives.
5. Claims, safety, intellectual property, or platform rules blocked the channel.

For each failure mode, identify the earliest signal and the cheapest test that can reveal it.
