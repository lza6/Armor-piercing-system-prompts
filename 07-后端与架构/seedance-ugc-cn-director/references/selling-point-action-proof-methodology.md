# Selling Point Action Proof Methodology

Use this methodology whenever writing apparel scripts, canonical timelines, B-roll lists, or Seedance/PopBoom prompts. Its job is to start from the customer's real wearing anxiety or purchase hesitation, select a selling point only when it solves that pain, and turn it into a physically executable, realistic human action that visibly proves the claim.

This file refines, but does not replace, `quality-directing-contract.md`, `motion-realism-and-commerce-cadence.md`, and `sku-batch-timeline-contract.md`. Evidence rules still come from `amazon-product-review-evidence-contract.md`.

## 1. Core Principle

Every selling point must become:

`one buyer pain or purchase hesitation -> one desired wearing result -> one evidence-backed claim -> one garment part or wearing effect -> one matched frame -> one simple action -> one visible endpoint -> one concise spoken line`

Do not write a selling point as an abstract statement and then add random movement for realism. The action is the proof. Micro-movements only make the creator feel alive; they never count as product proof.

Visible attributes are not automatically selling points. Color, neckline, sleeve, hem, length, print, crochet, knit, rib, ruffles, pockets, buttons, and seams become sellable only after they answer a buyer concern such as looking wider, boxy, childish, cheap, exposed, clingy, too plain, hard to style, or wrong for the occasion. If the analysis only says what viewers can already see, it is not deep enough for conversion.

Reject these patterns:

- `naturally shows the garment`
- `moves dynamically`
- `keeps gesturing while talking`
- `shows the details with hand movements`
- `walks, turns, pulls, points, and talks at the same time`
- any proof beat where the spoken line names one part but the camera/action shows another part

## 2. Pain-First Selling-Point Discovery

Before creating action-proof rows, identify candidate customer pains:

- body/proportion anxiety: waist not visible, hips look wider, legs look shorter, stomach/arm/upper-body area feels exposed, garment looks boxy or shapeless;
- age/style anxiety: too childish, too mature, too basic, too loud, cheap-looking, costume-like, or too revealing for the intended setting;
- fit/security anxiety: too tight, clingy, loose in the wrong place, mini length feels unsafe, neckline or armhole feels risky, fabric looks heavy or flimsy;
- styling/occasion anxiety: hard to match with real bottoms/shoes/bags, only good for photos, not usable for daily, vacation, office-casual, brunch, date, or errands;
- expectation anxiety from reviews or category habits: buyer expected a smoother, softer, more structured, longer, shorter, lighter, or more covered garment.

Then translate each viable pain into a desired wearing result. Examples:

- `makes the waist read smaller through waistline and skirt contrast`;
- `mini length but secure enough to move`;
- `sweet color without looking childish`;
- `loose but still shaped`;
- `covered at the arm but still light`;
- `texture detail makes a basic item look intentional`;
- `easy one-outfit styling instead of a hard-to-match statement piece`.

Only keep a result when it passes all four questions:

- Does the buyer care enough for this to affect purchase?
- Does the garment visibly or evidentially support the result?
- Can a 15-second video prove it with front/detail/side-back/action/styling angles?
- Does it differentiate the item from ordinary same-category products?

Qualified high-conversion language is allowed when the proof supports it. Use visual wearing-result wording such as `waist looks defined`, `makes the waist read smaller`, `leg line looks longer`, `not clingy at the hips`, `sweet but not childish`, `mini length but secure`, or `polished instead of cheap`. Do not promise actual body change, universal fit, comfort, softness, stretch, opacity, breathability, or performance without eligible evidence.

## 3. Pre-Script Action-Proof Mapping

Before writing the script, create one action-proof row for every selected selling point:

- `pain_point_id`: the buyer pain, purchase hesitation, or wearing anxiety being solved.
- `desired_wearing_result`: the conversion result the buyer wants.
- `claim_id`: exact allowlisted claim.
- `evidence_ids`: exact source items supporting the claim.
- `proof_target`: one canonical garment part or one wearing effect.
- `framing_class`: `detail_closeup`, `chest_to_hem`, `side_back`, `full_fit`, or `styling`.
- `action_type`: one executable action from the action selector below.
- `hands_required`: `one`, `two`, or `body`.
- `human_motivation`: why the creator naturally performs this action for the viewer now.
- `persistent_task_anchor`: filming hand, held prop, bag, pocket, garment edge, or prior endpoint that must remain occupied/continuous.
- `handoff_from_prior`: how the previous endpoint becomes this start state, or the explicit place-down/handoff/cut that makes a discontinuity credible.
- `spoken_line_shape`: target-language short line that names the target plus visible behavior or endpoint.
- `visible_endpoint`: the readable final state after the action.
- `micro_cues`: one or two supportive human cues when face/body is visible.

`human_motivation`, `persistent_task_anchor`, and `handoff_from_prior` are planning annotations. Compile their visible consequences into existing camera, action, hand-plan, endpoint, and cut language; do not add undeclared keys to a strict machine object unless the active schema explicitly supports them.

If a selling point cannot be traced back to a buyer pain and mapped to a simple visible action and endpoint, remove it from the 15-second script or downgrade it to non-spoken internal context. Do not solve a weak proof by adding adjectives.

## 4. Action Selector

Choose actions by proof target, not by variety.

| Proof target | Preferred frame | Preferred action | Visible endpoint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neckline, collar, V-neck, crew neck | detail close-up or chest-to-hem | `point_trace`: one hand lightly traces or taps the neckline once | neckline shape is unobstructed and readable |
| Shoulder line, sleeve cap | chest-to-hem or upper-body medium | `touch_release`: one hand touches the seam or sleeve edge once and returns | shoulder/sleeve placement remains visible |
| Sleeve length, cuff, arm coverage | sleeve close-up or upper-body medium | `cuff_touch` or `raise_arm`: touch cuff once, or raise one arm slowly | cuff position or coverage is clear |
| Fabric texture, knit, rib, surface detail | detail close-up, mouth out of frame | `pinch_release`: one small fold is pinched once and released | texture remains visible as fabric settles |
| Soft drape, fluid fall, non-cling visual | waist-to-hem, side, or chest-to-hem | `hem_release`, `smooth_release`, or `walk_settle`: release the hem or take one short motivated step and let the fabric settle | fabric falls into natural folds |
| Hem, length, crop, tunic length | waist-to-hem or full-fit | `hem_smooth_release` or `step_back` | hem line is clear relative to waist/hips/legs |
| Side slit, back coverage, side profile | side/back frame | `turn_settle`: quarter-turn or half-turn with weight transfer | side/back silhouette settles and is readable |
| Waist shaping, front structure | waist-to-hem or full-fit | `front_tuck`, `smooth_release`, or one small waist touch | waist relationship is visible without blocking garment |
| Pocket | detail or waist-to-hip | `pocket_use`: one hand enters or taps pocket once | pocket placement/opening is visible |
| Button, zipper, drawstring, closure | detail close-up | `open_close`: operate once from open to close or close to open | closure state is clearly finished |
| Outfit styling | full-fit | one `style_adjust`: front tuck, bag placement, necklace touch, shoe reveal, or step-back | complete outfit relationship is readable |
| Movement comfort or fit security | full-fit or side frame | `walk_settle`, `sit_settle`, `raise_arm`, or `turn_settle` | garment stays in intended position after movement |
| Stretch, recovery, opacity, cooling, water resistance, anti-wrinkle | only if eligible evidence allows performance demo | minimal evidence-matched action; two-hand pull only for supported stretch | visible result matches exact evidence scope |

When the evidence only shows a visible construction detail, use visible language and visible actions. Do not convert it into comfort, softness, breathability, slimming, cooling, or stretch unless the eligible evidence explicitly supports that claim.

## 5. Complete Beat Grammar

Write every proof beat in this order:

1. `start_state`: frame, body orientation, planted foot or weight-bearing leg, relaxed posture, gaze, current garment position, and the state inherited from the prior endpoint.
2. `left_hand_start`: exact stable anchor before movement.
3. `right_hand_start`: exact stable anchor before movement.
4. `main_action`: one action aimed at one proof target.
5. `motion_path`: short motivated physical trajectory from the start anchor to the target.
6. `visible_endpoint`: finished product state the viewer must read.
7. `left_hand_end`: stable final anchor.
8. `right_hand_end`: stable final anchor.
9. `human_micro_cues`: one or two subtle cues only when they do not compete with proof.
10. `handoff`: motivated continuation from the endpoint, or an explicit place-down, transfer, reset, or intentional cut when continuity cannot be preserved.

Do not hide multiple actions inside one sentence with `while`, `and`, or a list of verbs. A touch-return, pinch-release, pull-release, smooth-release, or turn-settle cycle counts as one action only when it has one target and one endpoint.

## 6. Realistic Human Kinetics

Use concrete biomechanics instead of generic realism:

- hands start from persistent task anchors or the prior endpoint whenever possible: phone, held prop, side seam, waist, pocket edge, hem, or relaxed side. Hip is an occasional natural anchor, not the automatic reset position;
- lead arm movement from the shoulder, then elbow, wrist, and fingers follow as one connected chain;
- keep wrists neutral, fingers softly grouped, elbows slightly relaxed, and shoulders naturally uneven by a small amount;
- for turns and steps, shift weight through hips and supporting leg before the body rotates or steps;
- for hand-only detail proof, keep feet planted and torso quiet;
- fabric reacts a fraction after the hand or body moves, then settles into believable folds before the cut;
- use natural ease-in and ease-out, never constant-speed limb motion or instant starts/stops.
- carry gaze, weight, facial state, hand occupancy, prop ownership, and garment state across adjacent beats. Do not snap to attention or return both hands to symmetric hip anchors between proofs;
- in a mirror selfie, one filming hand continuously owns the phone during a continuous take. An alternate-color garment, bag, or accessory remains in the declared hand until an explicit place-down, handoff, or cut;
- motivate distance changes by proof need: step back for full fit, move/turn for fabric or silhouette behavior, and walk closer for a decisive detail. Avoid centimeter/degree puppeteering unless exact evidence requires it.

Micro-cues should be sparse and causal:

- face visible: natural blink, tiny head tilt, small eyebrow lift, mouth-corner change, or brief glance from camera to garment and back;
- body visible: soft breath, small weight shift, relaxed shoulder asymmetry;
- close-up: slight finger pressure change, small fabric rustle, hand returns to an anchor.

Micro-cues support realism but do not replace the required product/styling action.

## 7. Speech Binding

Every claim-proof action needs one concise spoken unit, normally off-screen voiceover for detail or movement proof.

Across the whole clip, those units must sound like one connected recommendation. Carry the same social intention from buyer recognition through animated proof to conviction, using idiomatic live-share connectors and varied sentence openings rather than isolated equal-energy feature calls.

Good line shape:

`[buyer concern or desired result] + [target] + [visible behavior/position/result]`

Examples:

- `The cuff sits loose when I lift my arm.`
- `You can see the knit texture when I pinch and release it.`
- `The hem falls back into one clean line.`
- `Beim Drehen bleibt der Saum sichtbar.`

Avoid:

- generic praise: `looks nice`, `very comfortable`, `premium quality`;
- catalog wording: `this garment features`, `this product offers`;
- unsupported experience: `I bought/tested/washed this` without real evidence or user authorization;
- one spoken line naming several unrelated claims.
- feature-only lines that the viewer can infer without conversion logic, such as `It is pink`, `It has crochet`, or `It has a crew neck`, unless the line explains the buyer pain that feature resolves.

When the model's mouth is visible, keep the line short, reduce hand load, and bind one explicit micro-expression to the meaning of the line. Require visible emotional progression across the clip rather than one fixed smile. When the action is complex or the frame is a close-up, use off-screen voiceover and keep the mouth out of frame. When `secondary_fidelity_spend: lip_sync`, the quality contract may allow up to three concise visible anchors—one per macro performance phase—under its stricter camera, performer, hand-load, and word-budget conditions.

## 8. Prompt Rendering Pattern

For every Seedance/PopBoom shot block, render the action-proof row as visible direction:

```text
Shot [time]: [framing and proof target]. Motivation/handoff: [why this action happens now; prior endpoint or explicit place-down/handoff/cut]. Start: [body posture, gaze, left hand anchor, right hand anchor, persistent phone/prop ownership]. Action: [one hand/body action] from [start anchor] to [target] along [short motivated path]. Endpoint: [visible garment result], decelerate and let it read. Continuity: [left/right hand ends, gaze/weight/prop/garment state carried into the next beat]. Performance: [line-matched micro-expression and current emotional phase]. Motion: natural ease-in/ease-out, shoulder-elbow-wrist follow-through, relaxed fingers, subtle breathing/blink if face is visible, realistic fabric lag and settling. Speech: [speech mode], [exact line or none], [emphasis/pause intent]. Light/sound: [physical light source], [fabric rustle/room tone/footstep/etc.].
```

Keep this compact in the final prompt. Do not paste internal evidence IDs or analysis into Seedance; only include the visible consequence, exact spoken line, reference contract, camera/light, and compact constraints.

## 9. Example Transformations

Weak selling point:

```text
The dress has layered ruffles and crochet.
```

Pain-first action-proof rewrite:

```text
Buyer pain: a pink mini dress can look childish or too body-hugging. Desired result: waist looks defined while the skirt feels playful, not clingy. Full-fit frame. The model stands three-quarter to camera, weight on left leg. Left hand rests at the side seam. Right hand lightly touches the high waist seam once, then steps back so the layered skirt opens and settles. Endpoint: the waist seam and skirt flare create a clear waist-to-skirt contrast. Voiceover: "The waist reads smaller because the skirt opens out here."
```

Weak selling point:

```text
The sleeves are comfortable and flattering.
```

Action-proof rewrite:

```text
Upper-body sleeve frame. The creator remains three-quarter to camera with weight on the left leg from the prior fit beat. The left hand stays loosely at the side. The right hand travels from the prior hem endpoint, touches the sleeve cuff once, then settles near the side seam ready for the next proof. Endpoint: cuff and sleeve opening remain unobstructed; the creator gives one natural blink and a small relaxed smile. Voiceover: "The cuff sits loose without covering the shape."
```

Weak selling point:

```text
The fabric has nice drape.
```

Action-proof rewrite:

```text
Waist-to-hem frame. Left hand stays anchored at the side seam. Right hand lifts the front hem about 2 cm, releases it, and returns to the side. Endpoint: the hem falls back and settles into soft vertical folds. Voiceover: "The hem drops back into a clean line."
```

Weak selling point:

```text
Easy to style for daily wear.
```

Action-proof rewrite:

```text
Full-fit mirror frame. The model starts with both feet planted, left hand holding a small bag at the side, right hand at the front hem. Right hand makes one small front tuck, then drops to the side. Endpoint: the garment, jeans, shoes, and bag read as one complete outfit. Voiceover: "With high-waist jeans, the hem still stays visible."
```

## 10. Audit Checklist

Before finalizing a script or prompt, every selected selling point must pass:

- What real buyer pain or hesitation does this point solve?
- Is the selected result a deeper wearing outcome rather than a visible feature label?
- Does the spoken line name exactly one target or wearing result?
- Does the frame show that exact target large enough to read?
- Is there exactly one main action and one visible endpoint?
- Are left and right hand starts, paths, and end anchors clear?
- Does the prior endpoint naturally become the next start, with any phone/prop continuously owned until an explicit handoff or cut?
- Is every step, turn, push-in, or walk-closer motivated by the proof the viewer needs?
- Does the body have a plausible weight-bearing state for any turn, step, or sit?
- Do micro-cues support the proof instead of replacing it?
- Does fabric lag or settle after contact or body movement?
- Is the action allowed by the evidence scope?
- Is the line casual and spoken, not catalog copy?
- Do the lines form one friend-to-friend recommendation with a recognition -> proof -> conviction energy rise rather than flat isolated calls?
- Do visible-face speaking beats use line-matched and meaningfully varied micro-expressions?
- Does the clip still deliver a completed product/styling proof roughly every 2-3 seconds?

If any answer is no, repair the row before writing the final Seedance/PopBoom prompt.
