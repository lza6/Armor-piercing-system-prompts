Use this reference whenever writing apparel hooks, section 7 scripts, section 9 Seedance/PopBoom prompts, section 12 captions, or optional repaired prompts when the user explicitly asks for a separate repair task.

This library supplies reusable creative forms beneath the market compiler:

- conversion-oriented PopBoom apparel UGC base formats for first-generation scripts;
- stability structures for first-generation prompts, plus optional repair structures only for user-requested repair tasks.

Do not use this file as the source of market or scene-routing logic. Read `market-prompt-compiler-contract.md` and `occasion-scene-routing.md`, then only the selected `us-winning-prompt-profile.md` or `de-winning-prompt-profile.md`, before choosing a base template. Do not copy template wording mechanically. ## Selection Rules

Before writing the script:

1. Identify garment type, color, neckline, sleeve, length, drape, texture, coverage, and visible details from the product or clothing three-view reference. When an Amazon URL exists, lock the parent/child ASIN and selected variant before using page facts. Treat this as evidence intake, not the final selling-point list.
2. Map buyer pain points to independently evidenced solution claims and visible product proof. Start from the buyer's real wearing anxiety or purchase hesitation before naming features. Review-derived pains require direct review IDs or a valid theme; seller copy and summaries cannot create buyer consensus. A complaint with no evidenced solution stays risk-only.
3. Select one `core_sellable_wearing_result`: the single wearing outcome that answers the strongest buyer hesitation and can be proven visually in 15 seconds. Do this before choosing a template. Do not use a fixed category formula such as always making dresses about waist, oversized tops about comfort, or pants about fabric; let buyer worry plus visible garment evidence decide.
4. Select `us_champion_v1` or `de_champion_v1`, persist all scalar `generation_controls`, and lock the complete occasion-first `scene_strategy` before choosing a base form. Never translate a finished template execution between markets.
5. Choose the base template and timeline grid from the scene's sales job and proof need. Reuse proof-coverage obligations and stability rules across a same-SKU partition, not complete shot content or one habitual location.
6. Apply `quality-directing-contract.md`, `motion-realism-and-commerce-cadence.md`, and `selling-point-action-proof-methodology.md`: choose one directing intent, role-lock the apparel reference, allocate garment fidelity first, cap actual camera setups/motion load, and turn every selected selling point into one matched frame, one proof action, one complete start-action-end hand path, motivated camera/light, sparse micro-cues, one `proof_endpoint`, and one market-language line naming the same target.
7. Apply the relevant stability template as an audit pass before finalizing the Seedance/PopBoom prompt, even for first-generation scripts.
8. Keep recent-half-month TikTok apparel-commerce research above template habits but below identity/evidence/safety and the market compiler. Research informs the current creative form; it never proves a claim or changes the market-language contract.
9. For German/Germany output, apply the local TikTok Studio account learning from `nanettefei5` only after `de_champion_v1`: the account overlay may optimize profile/shop/comment action but cannot change the German speech, camera, evidence, or CTA-purity contract.

Evidence language rules:

- Never generate a fake first-person purchase, try-on, wash, or test experience. Use “I tried/bought/tested” only with real test evidence or explicit user authorization.
- One direct review may be attributed only to one buyer. “In the reviews sampled” requires at least three unique review IDs; never imply the full rating population was read.
- Product attributes, seller claims, buyer experience, and buyer-image observations must stay separate in the wording.
- Conflicted care/material/size/stretch evidence and mixed review themes cannot become absolute promises.
- A buyer image can inspire a visible fit/length/construction shot only after actual inspection; it never proves feel, comfort, composition, stretch, breathability, care, or durability.

## Core Sellable Wearing Result Discovery

Use this pass for every new garment before writing hooks, timelines, prompts, or captions. The goal is not to list product features; it is to find the one visible wearing result most likely to make the buyer click.

1. List likely buyer hesitations for the garment type and visible silhouette. Examples: loose tops may look sloppy or boxy; fitted tops may cling or show the stomach; pants may pull at hips, shorten legs, or lose drape; jackets may look bulky; mini lengths may feel insecure; textured pieces may look cheap or ordinary.
2. Match each hesitation to eligible product evidence and a visible solution claim. A result is eligible only if the garment can show it through a simple proof action or angle.
3. Score each candidate result: buyer relevance, visual provability, differentiation from ordinary same-category products, and 15-second proof feasibility.
4. Lock exactly one primary result for the video. Secondary details may appear only as proof for that result.

Hook and proof rules:

- Write the hook as the result or the resolved hesitation, not as a product label. Prefer concise buyer language such as `Oversized, but not sloppy.`, `Wide-leg, but it does not swallow my height.`, `Warm, but not bulky.`, `Mini length, but it still feels secure.`, or `This waist detail actually shapes the romper.` Adapt naturally to the market and evidence.
- Do not write hooks or proof lines that only restate obvious features like color, crochet, ruffles, neckline, sleeve, hem, print, or pockets. The line must explain the buyer outcome those details create, such as waist definition, less cling, more secure coverage, less childish sweetness, better proportion, or easier styling.
- The first frame must already show the result or the buyer-worry angle. Do not start with a neutral product introduction.
- Build the 15-second proof stack as `result reveal -> structural reason -> alternate angle/buyer-worry proof -> action endpoint -> outfit/use-case -> evidence-safe verdict -> optional profile-authorized CTA`.
- Valid proof actions include one controlled pull-release, hem release, side/back turn, seated/step proof, arm raise, closure operation, front tuck, bag/necklace placement, or hand trace of the relevant part. Pick actions because they prove the selected result, not because the template needs motion. Use `selling-point-action-proof-methodology.md` to choose the action by proof target: neckline claims need neckline trace/tap, sleeve claims need cuff touch or arm raise, texture claims need close-up pinch-release, drape claims need hem release or smooth-release, side/back claims need turn-settle, and styling claims need one outfit action. Do not use pull-release, two-hand stretch, opacity, cooling, water-resistance, or anti-wrinkle demonstrations unless the exact evidence allows that performance proof.
- Remove a true detail when it does not support the primary result. More features do not equal more conversion.

### Evidence-Backed Negative Contrast Hook

Use this only when a direct-review/review-theme material-expectation pain is `script_eligible` and maps to an independent exact-variant visible `texture` claim. Prefer the declarative contrast over a softer question when it sounds natural in the target language.

- Hook pattern: negative expectation contrast, such as Chinese `这可不是普通的光滑T恤——近看这个细针织纹理。`, German `Kein glattes Standard-T-Shirt – sieh dir die feine Strickstruktur an.`, or English `Not a basic tee—look at this fine knit texture.` Adapt idiomatically; do not translate word for word when the result sounds stiff.
- Keep `spoken_claim_ids: []`, the selected `pain_point_id`, and the exact review-pain evidence on the Hook.
- Make the immediately following beat the mapped texture proof: realistic phone-camera detail close-up, mouth out of frame, off-screen voiceover, one hand pinches one small fold once and releases, and the texture settles visibly.
- Do not place a reaction, full-fit explanation, styling beat, or second product claim between the Hook and texture proof.
- Do not use this structure for category inference, seller copy, a blocked/risk-only complaint, mismatched variants, or unsupported softness, comfort, breathability, stretch, composition, or durability claims.

## Quality Direction Pass

Before a template populates the canonical timeline:

- write one `directing_intent` as the buyer response the video must create;
- lock the selected market profile, all seven scalar `generation_controls`, and `scene_strategy`; the template may not override language, delivery, camera, music, verdict, commerce CTA, screen-text mode, wear context, occasion, buyer styling question, outfit answer, location plan, or private-interior policy;
- write one continuous `creator_social_intention` and shape the viewer-facing performance as three macro phases—recognition/result Hook, animated proof, and close/detail conviction—without reducing the required five to seven internal proof beats;
- set garment identity as the primary fidelity spend and select only one secondary spend;
- bind the three-view to garment identity only and positively replace its white triptych source layout with one creator in one continuous real-world scene;
- map the commercial purposes into three to four contiguous camera-setup runs and five to seven sequential beats in 15 seconds;
- require at least four non-CTA product/styling demonstration actions and at least three distinct part/effect proofs;
- give each beat one action, one visible endpoint, `proof_endpoint` for a proof beat, `spoken_language`, motivated camera/light, explicit left/right hand trajectories, one specific sound intent, and a natural handoff from the prior endpoint; use exact coordinates only when product proof requires them;
- run the selling-point action-proof pass: one claim -> one proof target -> one frame -> one action -> one endpoint -> one concise spoken line, with explicit hand anchors and micro-cues that support rather than replace the proof;
- maintain native commerce cadence with a newly completed product proof or styling result about every two to three seconds; use natural ease-in/ease-out, shoulder-elbow-wrist follow-through, plausible weight transfer, and garment lag/settling rather than constant-speed movement or slow fashion posing;
- preserve persistent task anchors and object ownership. A mirror-selfie filming hand keeps the phone; an alternate-color garment or accessory stays in the declared hand until an explicit place-down, handoff, or intentional cut. Do not reset both hands to the hips or return to attention posture between proofs;
- direct a restrained emotional rise from warm recognition to animated certainty to delighted conviction or relief, and write the voice as one connected friend-to-friend recommendation with varied emphasis and proof-pivot pauses;
- reduce camera/body/hand load for visible lip-sync, detail proof, and CTA;
- make adjacent beats share a setup when the same frame can prove the next target; reframe or cut whenever a different garment part needs its own close-up.

Templates never override this pass. A fashionable angle, extra transition, accessory action, or friend line must be removed when it competes with garment identity, the full claim-proof sequence, anatomy, or lip-sync. A designated hero proof receives priority but never replaces the other required part/effect proofs.
The selected `core_sellable_wearing_result` is the commercial throughline. A template may change scene, pacing, or speaking style, but it may not turn the video into equal-weight coverage of unrelated garment features.

## Base Template A: Friend-Reaction Try-On

Use when the garment benefits from a conversational, high-retention UGC setup.

Structure:

- Opening, about 0-2.5s: product-visible friend reaction or buyer-problem hook with one natural posture/fit-reveal action.
- Proof sequence, about 2.5-11.5s: three or more short claim-matched proof beats. Give every named garment part its own readable frame and action; keep the mouth out of frame and use off-screen voiceover for controlled demonstrations.
- Styling and close, about 11.5-15s: perform one simple styling/fit action, show the result, deliver the selected market verdict, then include a short CTA and one real human gesture only when `commerce_cta_mode` requires it.

Voiceover logic:

- Hook with a buyer problem or outfit reaction, not a generic product intro.
- Move through fit proof, visible detail, styling imagination, verdict, and any profile-authorized CTA.
- Normally use five to six short spoken units for 15 seconds, with one concise matching line for every claim-proof action. Default to no more than two visible-lip lines; use up to three only when lip-sync is the declared secondary fidelity spend and the conditional camera/performer/hand/word budgets pass. Carry complex detail proof with off-screen voiceover.
- Keep every line tied to a visible shot.
- Write for the mouth: short everyday sentences, varied openings, no seller-page constructions, official transitions, or generic filler. Do not start three or more proof/styling lines with the same phrase.
- When evidence permits, make the non-CTA information units cover four proof angles for the selected result: the visible result, the structural reason, another angle that answers a buyer worry, and one specific outfit/use-case relationship. Each line names one target and what it visibly does or where it sits; one beat still carries only one claim.

## Base Template B: Mirror Try-On Proof

Use only after the private-interior gate passes, for garments where fit, length, drape, front/side/back view, or multi-styling is the dominant proof. It is not the default for tops, dresses, sets, casual basics, or outward wear merely because a mirror is generation-stable. For an outward-use item, prefer the proof-only opening plus one transition to the real destination when that better answers the buyer.

Structure:

- `0-4s — recognition/result Hook`: show the phone and filming hand in the mirror, keep the worn garment visible, and name the buyer need or desired everyday result in one direct friend-to-friend line. If an alternate color is used, declare which free hand holds it. Step back only because the viewer needs a clearer fit view.
- `4-10s — animated proof`: continue the same selfie action instead of restarting a pose. Use two or three internal beats—such as a short forward/back walk, weight shift, quarter-turn, drape release, or side view—to prove the selected fit/material behavior. Each internal beat still has one target and endpoint, but gaze, weight, phone hand, free hand, prop, and garment state carry forward.
- `10-15s — close/detail conviction`: if a prop must leave frame, place it down or cut explicitly; then walk closer or intentionally reframe because the viewer needs to inspect the decisive neckline, sleeve, texture, hem, or non-cling result. Finish with a sincere evidence-backed verdict and/or a short human-only CTA.

Performance rules:

- Treat the creator as someone excited to show a useful find to a friend, not a model completing isolated inspection poses.
- Keep the filming hand continuously on the phone during a continuous take. The free hand demonstrates only one target at a time; it starts from its previous endpoint or a relaxed natural anchor, never from a repeated symmetric hip reset.
- Motivate every distance change and let each action decelerate into a readable result. Do not choreograph ordinary creator behavior with unnecessary centimeters, degrees, or timed freezes.
- Use a visible emotional arc: warm recognition in the opening, brighter certainty while the garment proves itself in motion, and pleased conviction or relief in the close-up. Passion comes from changing emphasis and stakes, not uniform shouting or frantic movement.
- Claims such as softness, non-cling, comfort, or `my new favorite` require eligible evidence and identity-safe wording. When that evidence is absent, describe only the visible drape, space from the body, construction, or styling result actually shown.

Voiceover logic:

- Write one connected live-share thought, not six equal-energy catalog lines. Use idiomatic target-language connectors equivalent to `look`, `when I move`, `see how`, and `honestly` only when truthful.
- For a 15-second American-English creator-led mirror share, aim for roughly 50-60 total words and about 200-235 WPM only when a TTS/read-aloud audition remains clear. Use fewer words when articulation, language, or proof density needs more space.
- If `secondary_fidelity_spend: lip_sync`, up to three concise visible-dialogue anchors—one per macro phase—may be used with locked/subtle camera, simple performer load, one active demonstration hand, and an explicit expression cue. Otherwise keep complex proof off-screen and follow the default visible-lip budget.

Styling logic:

- Build a complete outfit around the item: bottoms, shoes, bag, simple jewelry, bracelet/watch, belt, sunglasses, or hair styling only when useful.
- Match scene palette to garment color. Avoid a background that makes the garment disappear.
- State the buyer's destination and outfit question even in a justified mirror proof. If the real-use scene would sell the garment better, route to the destination template instead.
- Accessories must not cover neckline, sleeve, hem, print, texture, or any key selling detail.

## Base Template C: Detail-Proof Close-Up

Use when fabric texture, stitching, neckline, sleeve, hem, buttons, pockets, print, or visible construction is the main selling proof.

Structure:

- Opening: product-visible hook naming one real buyer hesitation.
- When the hesitation is an eligible review-derived smooth-T-shirt-versus-visible-knit expectation gap, use the evidence-backed negative contrast Hook above and make texture `pinch_release` the first proof beat.
- Proof sequence: give every selected construction/material claim its own close-up and physical action. Sequence neckline trace, cuff touch, texture pinch-release, closure operation, hem release, or another evidence-backed demonstration as appropriate; never make several claims share one unrelated gesture.
- Worn-fit and close: perform one full-fit/side-view action, let the garment reach a readable endpoint, then deliver the profile-selected verdict and optional CTA.

Prompt safeguards:

- Do not claim exact composition, cooling technology, elasticity level, anti-wrinkle effect, or certified breathability unless an eligible research-bundle item supports it. Exact composition may use a locked catalog/label source; numeric performance still requires a verified test.
- A `Low Stretch` attribute means no strong pull-release. Use a texture pinch-release, drape release, seam/side-slit close-up, or fit turn instead.
- Use visible wording such as "looks lightweight", "has a soft drape", "you can see the texture", or the target-market equivalent.
- Keep close-ups realistic: a phone-camera push-in, not an unnatural microscope view.

## Base Template D: Color-And-Outfit Styling

Use for multi-color batches or colors where styling imagination is the conversion driver.

Structure:

- Opening: color-specific hook tied to one wardrobe problem or occasion; full garment visible with a natural fit-reveal action.
- Proof sequence: combine at least two garment-part demonstrations with one color-specific styling action, each in its own beat and matched frame.
- Final fit and close: complete the styling or side-view endpoint, allow a brief readable result, deliver the selected verdict, and perform a human-only lower-left gesture only for `light_link` or an authorized evidence-backed promo.

Multi-color material lock:

- Treat later colorways as the same garment with color changed only.
- Use identical fabric construction, knit/weave texture scale, thickness, opacity, drape, seams, sleeve construction, neckline, and hem across colorways.
- Use the positive baseline lock in first-generation prompts. Add one material-drift exclusion only when a prior take actually shows the named failure.

## Base Template E: Destination / Occasion Outfit Share

Use when the scene itself helps answer where and how to wear the garment: café or terrace, beach/boardwalk, commercial street, sidewalk, office/commute edge, travel hotel, entryway departure, or another evidence-appropriate real use case.

Structure:

- Opening: show the complete outfit and destination immediately. State the buyer's occasion-specific hesitation or desired result while the garment remains the focal point.
- Proof sequence: use location-native but controlled actions—arrival step and settle, bag pickup, seated-to-standing fit, shaded walk, quarter-turn, hem/drape movement, sleeve/closure check, or one outfit adjustment—only when each action proves the selected garment target.
- Styling answer: visibly connect the garment to the declared bottoms, shoes, bag, and restrained accessory formula. Do not let scenery or props replace garment proof.
- Close: hold the finished outfit in the primary use-case scene and give the evidence-safe verdict plus any profile-authorized human-only CTA.

US may use stronger café, beach, commercial-street, market, event-arrival, or travel energy when generation load remains solvable. Germany uses a calmer café terrace, pedestrian street, entryway, commute edge, hotel, or promenade treatment without landmarks or tourist signaling. In both markets, the destination must solve the styling question rather than act as decorative B-roll.

## Required 15-Second Motion-Rich Beat Pattern

Use this adaptable timing unless the user explicitly changes duration:

- `0-4s — recognition/result Hook`: garment visible immediately; name the buyer need/result and complete one motivated reveal, step-back, or posture-to-fit action.
- `4-10s — animated proof`: continue the creator's social intention while completing two or three internal proof beats across the structural, movement, angle, coverage, or handling evidence that matters most.
- `10-15s — close/detail conviction`: motivate a closer/reframed decisive proof, resolve the strongest remaining doubt, show the result, then deliver the selected market verdict and any profile-authorized CTA. A `light_link` uses one human-only gesture; `none` uses no link gesture. The screen stays clean except for the required persistent upper-left fit-stats overlay.

The three macro phases describe the viewer-perceived performance, not the internal compiler count. Exact boundaries may change, but every default 15-second result still needs five to seven sequential internal beats, at least four non-CTA demonstration actions, at least three part/effect proofs, and three to four contiguous camera-setup runs. Deliver a newly completed product proof or styling result about every two to three seconds. A brief endpoint hold is allowed inside an action beat; an entire explanatory beat may not consist of standing still.

Every timestamped beat should include:

- machine `scene_id` plus matching natural scene/framing;
- model action with start posture, left/right hand start anchors inherited from the prior endpoint when possible, one motivated motion path, visible endpoint, left/right hand end anchors, and natural continuation or intentional cut;
- speech mode, mouth visibility, lip-sync flag, and the canonical spoken line or explicit no-speech state;
- exact `spoken_language` and a concrete `proof_endpoint` for every proof beat;
- product point being shown;
- one or two realistic micro-cues only when face/body visibility makes them useful;
- lighting direction and believable shadow behavior when writing the final prompt.

### Conversational Voiceover Pass

After the evidence and action plan are locked, read every spoken line aloud. Rewrite any line that sounds like a catalog, press release, or translated seller bullet. Reject phrases such as `this garment features`, `this product offers`, `furthermore`, `moreover`, `in addition`, German `dieses Kleidungsstück verfügt über`, `darüber hinaus`, `des Weiteren`, and Chinese `本产品采用`, `该服装具备`, `此外`, `综上`. Reject standalone praise such as `looks nice`, `looks good`, `easy to style`, German `sieht gut aus` / `leicht zu kombinieren`, and Chinese `很好看` / `很百搭`.

Prefer target + visible behavior/position: `See how the side slit opens when I turn?` or German `Beim Drehen öffnet sich der Seitenschlitz.` Vary sentence shapes across the sequence; do not force slang or fake first-person purchase/testing history. Richer detail comes from covering more eligible concrete lenses across beats, not from packing several claims into one long line.

Then audition the full script as one performance. For a creator-led American-English 15-second mirror share, use 50-60 words and roughly 200-235 WPM as an aspirational conversion range only when every word remains intelligible; use the validator's broader safety range for hard gating. Mark proof-pivot pauses and emphasis words, and require the voice and visible expressions to progress from recognition through brighter proof energy to conviction. Reject equal stress, identical sentence endings, a fixed grin, or one flat emotional level.

Apply the selected delivery mode after this conversational pass:

- `us_hybrid_share`: Hook/close may be visible American-English dialogue; complex middle proof is off-screen voiceover with the mouth out of frame.
- `de_live_simple`: every visible German line must pass the low-load camera/performer/hand/lip-sync gate.
- `de_hybrid_proof`: Hook/close may be live German; the same creator carries the middle German off-screen voiceover while the mouth stays out of frame.

For German, bind `hier`, `so`, and `genau da` to the exact framed target and proof action. Reject Chinese or high-confidence English template residue in German spoken slots, while allowing verified brand/product/platform tokens through an explicit allowlist. For every market, project the exact spoken line plus a Chinese review translation into `voiceover_review`; never place that Chinese translation inside the generated prompt.

## Repair Template: CTA Visual Purity

Use when the generated video shows an arrow, arrow emoji, sticker, shopping cart, pointer, badge, UI overlay, floating icon, product-link graphic, or any extra symbol.

Repair pattern:

- Name the exact observed error in the revised prompt.
- Remove all wording that asks to "show", "point to", "display", "add", or "put" visual CTA aids.
- If the selected close uses `light_link`, replace the artifact with one small natural down-left hand gesture or brief down-left glance. If `commerce_cta_mode: none`, remove both the artifact and link gesture. In both cases, the screen stays clean except for the required persistent upper-left fit-stats overlay.
- Add only the exact observed CTA artifact as the negative constraint, plus the positive replacement of a clean screen and one real human gesture.

## Repair Template: Hands And Body Stability

Use when the video shows extra hands, missing hands, distorted fingers, detached wrists, merged arms, impossible actions, or unstable turns.

Repair pattern:

- Reduce each beat to one main action, but preserve several useful sequential garment actions across the video.
- Specify each hand's start anchor, action, and end anchor in every shot.
- Carry the previous endpoint into the next start state, including gaze, weight, hand occupancy, prop ownership, and garment state. Specify a relaxed start, natural ease-in/ease-out, plausible weight transfer, realistic garment lag/settling, and a readable endpoint; reset only when clarity requires it or use an intentional cut.
- Keep persistent task anchors stable: a selfie filming hand retains the phone and a held garment/accessory stays owned until an explicit place-down, handoff, or cut. Remove repeated attention posture and symmetric hip resets.
- Avoid asking the model to hold, pull, point, walk, turn, and visibly speak at the same time; move complex proof to off-screen voiceover instead of deleting the action.
- Prefer a sequence of correctly performed actions such as neckline trace, cuff touch, controlled pinch-release, hem smooth/release, slow half-turn, side/back reveal, and styling action, followed only by the profile-authorized close behavior.
- Add positive constraints: one person only, one head, two arms, two hands, five fingers per hand, natural wrists, hands attached to arms, normal shoulders.
- Add only the exact observed anatomy error as the negative constraint, plus the positive replacement of one creator with exactly two natural connected arms/hands and explicit sequential hand trajectories. Default to one active hand; allow a declared evidence-backed coordinated two-hand proof.

## Repair Template: Product And Material Consistency

Use when the garment changes color, shape, neckline, sleeve, hem, texture, drape, opacity, or construction across shots.

Repair pattern:

- Re-anchor the prompt to the clothing three-view reference.
- Re-anchor only the view/detail needed for the failed beat; do not stack front, side, and back into one repair shot.
- State the exact color and key visible details in every major beat.
- For same-garment multi-color batches, explicitly state: `same garment as the baseline colorway, color changed only; identical fabric construction, knit/weave texture scale, thickness, opacity, drape, seams, sleeve construction, neckline, and hem`.
- Add only the exact observed product/material drift as the negative constraint, plus the positive baseline-garment replacement.

## Repair Template: Scene, Face, Voice, And Lighting

Use when the model looks stiff, lighting is fake, scene jumps, body does not match scene, lip-sync fails, or voiceover feels robotic.

Repair pattern:

- Specify the exact location, scene objects, camera setup, model posture, and product placement.
- Specify light source and direction, soft shadows on face/body/fabric, natural highlights, and consistent shadows across cuts.
- Give the clip one continuous creator intention and three-phase energy curve: warm recognition, animated proof certainty, then delighted conviction or relief. Give each visible-face speaking beat one line-matched micro-expression and require at least two distinct expression states across the clip.
- If the mouth is visible, require lip-sync to the exact spoken words. When lip-sync is the declared secondary fidelity spend, keep no more than three visible anchors, each with locked/subtle camera, simple performer load, and one active demonstration hand; move complex proof off-screen.
- Keep speech conversational but commercially alive: connected phrasing, varied emphasis, short proof-pivot pauses, and a clear emotional rise. For 15-second American-English mirror shares, audition roughly 50-60 words at 200-235 WPM only when intelligible.
- Carry phone/prop ownership and prior endpoints across adjacent beats; motivate every step-back, turn, push-in, or walk-closer by the proof the viewer needs.
- Add only the exact observed scene/face/light error as the negative constraint, plus the positive replacement for creator posture, expression, and physical light.

## Repair Report Contract

Use this contract only when the user explicitly asks for a separate repair task for a failed PopBoom/Seedance video.

Required input fields:

- color/product;
- record_id when available;
- failure category;
- observed timestamp/frame;
- visible problem;
- likely prompt cause;
- exact correction needed;
- prior attempt count;
- whether the same issue appeared in other colors.

Failure categories:

- `CTA visual impurity`: arrow, shopping cart, sticker, emoji, pointer, badge, UI overlay, or product-link graphic.
- `anatomy/action instability`: three hands, extra hands, missing hands, distorted fingers, detached hands, merged limbs, impossible wrist/arm, or too many simultaneous actions.
- `product consistency`: wrong color, garment shape drift, neckline/sleeve/hem drift, product disappears, wrong reference, or product-body mismatch.
- `scene/model mismatch`: body-scene misalignment, face/body mismatch, wrong model style, lighting inconsistency, background jump, or over-polished commercial look.
- `voice/lip-sync/copy`: wrong spoken language, robotic pacing, sparse voiceover, visible mouth not matching words, weak hook, unsupported claims, or CTA too stiff.

Repair output must include:

- diagnosed failure category;
- preserved `market_prompt_contract_id`, `market_prompt_profile_id`, and `generation_controls`, unless the user explicitly authorizes a market-mode change and the package is recompiled as v6;
- exact prompt lines or beats changed;
- corrected full copy-ready prompt/script;
- the compact observed-failure constraint and positive replacement added;
- whether the repair is color-specific or should be reused as a shared batch pattern.
- the take verdict (`keep`, `fix_in_post`, `reroll`, `rewrite`, or `stop_or_rescope`) and one `primary_failure_variable`;
- the single variable changed for the authorized retake. Two takes with the same failure require rewrite/decomposition rather than another blind reroll.
